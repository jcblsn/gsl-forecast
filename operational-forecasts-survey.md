# Operational and Gray-Literature Forecasts of Great Salt Lake Level

Survey date: 2026-09-02. Numbers are quoted as published; "not stated" where absent. Elevations are south arm (USGS 10010000, Saltair Boat Harbor) in feet unless noted.

## 1. NOAA/NWS Colorado Basin River Forecast Center (CBRFC)

- CBRFC does not publish a Great Salt Lake elevation forecast. Its GSL-relevant products are seasonal (Apr-Jul) unregulated water-supply volume forecasts for the tributaries, grouped under sub-area "GL" (Bear, Weber, Provo, Six Creeks, etc.): https://www.cbrfc.noaa.gov/rmap/wsup/wsuplist.php?rfc=cbrfc&sort=Area&search=&match=&search2=&match2=&showplot=0&off=DS-P.30-Avg&search=Sub.Area&match=GL
- Method (https://www.cbrfc.noaa.gov/wsup/doc/doc.html; April 2026 briefing https://www.cbrfc.noaa.gov/present/2026/cbrfcwsupapr2026.pdf): Ensemble Streamflow Prediction. Current model snow and soil-moisture states are run forward with 30 historical weather years (1991-2020), yielding exceedance volumes (10/50/90%). Inputs: SNOTEL SWE, CBRFC gridded model SWE, model soil moisture, QPF. Official issue monthly Jan-Jun; daily raw guidance.
- Verification (April-issued Apr-Jul volume, average absolute error, from the same briefing): Weber-Oakley 18%, Provo-Woodland 16%, Big Cottonwood Creek 16%. No lake-level verification.
- April 2026 values: Bear River basin 56 kaf (51% avg, rank 3/84), Weber 61 kaf (55%), Provo 50 kaf (52%), Big Cottonwood 15.4 kaf (45%).
- NWS AHPS gauge GSLU1 (https://water.noaa.gov/gauges/gslu1) is observation-only (page currently redirects; no forecast confirmed).
- The special-products page (https://www.cbrfc.noaa.gov/product/hydrofcst/hydrofcst.php) lists no GSL product.

## 2. USDA NRCS Utah Snow Survey: the only operational GSL level outlook

- Publisher: NRCS Utah Snow Survey (Jordan Clayton), in the monthly Utah Water Supply Outlook Report (WSOR), Jan-May. Index: https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/state-offices/utah/water-supply-outlook-reports. GSL resource page: https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/state-offices/utah/the-great-salt-lake (NWCC chart of contributing-basin inflow forecast exceedance probabilities; GSL basin SWE, precipitation, soil moisture from contributing SNOTEL sites; USGS inflow gauge map).
- Target: (a) GSL inflow volume Apr-Jul (May-Jul from the April report) at 10/30/50/70/90% exceedance; (b) lake rise from report date to seasonal peak; (c) implied peak stage. Horizon: 1-6 months. First issued in WY2024.
- Method: statistical regression on SNOTEL SWE and precipitation (NRCS standard) for a "GSL inflow" forecast point (Weber, Provo, Bear watersheds only; "other subbasins do not substantively contribute"; 38 observation years on the Feb 2025 chart), converted to lake rise. Explicitly "advisory-only"; does not account for diversions, reservoir operations, berm management, or evaporation.

## 3. Utah DWR / GSLBIP / GSLIM (planning models, not forecasts)

- Great Salt Lake Integrated Model (GSLIM): GoldSim model built by Jacobs/CH2M (2017) for the GSL Advisory Council; river-basin modules (Bear, Weber, Jordan; 38 sub-basins), wetland modules, lake module with four bays; outputs water surface elevation and salinity. https://www.goldsim.com/Web/Applications/ExampleApplications/EnvironmentalExamples/GSLIM/. Calibration statistics: not stated.
- 2019 GSLAC scenario study (https://documents.deq.utah.gov/water-quality/standards-technical-services/great-salt-lake-advisory-council/activities/DWQ-2019-015344.pdf): start 4,192.3 ft (1 Jan 2019, NGVD29), simulated 2019-2051 with repeated 1981-2013 climate adjusted per CMIP5 median (+1.4 C / +3.3% precipitation by 2030; +2.8 C / +6.2% by 2060). South-arm change vs baseline: Baseline Future -3.3 ft (2030 growth) / -3.8 ft (2060); State Water Strategy -1.9 / -2.7; Adaptive Innovation -1.2 / -1.8; Hot Growth -11.7 / -12.8. States GSLIM "cannot predict the future"; recommends developing 5-10-year lake level forecasts.
- GSL Basin Integrated Plan (DWR + Reclamation WaterSMART Basin Study; HB 429): https://water.utah.gov/gsl-basin-integrated-plan/; modeling approach https://water.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/GSLBIP-Work-Plan-Section-C.pdf. Mid-term tool: VIC rainfall-runoff model driven by MACA-downscaled CMIP6 (average / hottest-driest / coolest-wettest), consolidated RiverWare river-operations model, empirical groundwater model, upgraded GSLIM (USU causeway DLL, evaporation, salinity), demand models. Due 30 Nov 2026; scenario analyses summary Dec 2025, draft plan Jul-Sep 2026. No public lake-level projections from the updated tool found yet. Study status: https://water.utah.gov/gslbip-studies/ (evaporation quantification study due June 2026).
- DWR elevation page (https://water.utah.gov/great-salt-lake-elevation/) and DNR current conditions (https://greatsaltlake.utah.gov/current-conditions/) are observation dashboards. DWR notes a typical spring rise of "about 2 feet"; 2023 rise 5.5 ft.
- Utah Geological Survey: no lake-level projection product found.

## 4. USGS

No operational elevation forecast. Products: real-time elevation/inflow/salinity Hydro Mapper (https://webapps.usgs.gov/gsl/); 2023 topobathymetric DEM and elevation-area-volume tables (https://www.usgs.gov/centers/utah-water-science-center/science/great-salt-lake-elevations-and-areal-extent; data DOI 10.5066/P9DGG75W); historical water/salt-balance model Loving, Waddell and Miller (2000), WRI 2000-4221, https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/wri004221 (1987-98 water and salt balance plus causeway flow simulation; verification numbers not stated on the landing page).

## 5. University-based projections

- Utah Climate Center (USU) "GSL Annual Level Prediction", https://climate.usu.edu/GSL.php. Target: annual lake level, 5-8 years ahead. Method: principal-component lagged regression of annual lake-level tendency on NINO4 SST (Kaplan), GSL-watershed COOP precipitation, and lake level, plus a tree-ring ARX variant using the DeRose et al. 576-yr reconstruction. Skill (Gillies et al. 2011, https://doi.org/10.1175/2010jhm1352.1): in-sample RMSE 0.86-0.90 ft on annual tendency, 1.2-1.5 ft on elevation (1943-2007); out-of-sample 8-year-ahead RMSE mean 3.02 ft (PCLag) vs about 4 ft (PLag). Gillies et al. 2015 (https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs_journals/2015/rmrs_2015_gillies_r001.pdf): 5-yr-ahead tendency RMSE averaged 40.2 cm/yr (obs-only AR), 35.6 and 32.1 cm/yr (TreeARX), vs 32.8 cm/yr standard deviation of annual tendency. Known multi-decadal wet/dry bias. Current numeric forecast values: graph only, not stated in text.
- GSL Strike Team (U of U Gardner Institute + USU), Jan 2026 Data and Insights Summary: https://d36oiwf74r1rap.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/GSL-Jan2026-Final.pdf; data and code https://www.hydroshare.org/resource/885979f4acdd412ab3cf09799eab7ead/ (2024 version https://www.hydroshare.org/resource/ea98db4caa1141cdbae92606f79f6909/). Method (Tarboton): annual water-balance model in R (GSLRmodel2.R) with USGS area-volume tables; 1,000 Monte Carlo realizations resampling annual inflow, precipitation (PRISM/Gridmet) and evaporation from 2000-2025; 30-yr horizon from 4,191 ft (2025) to 2055. Scenarios: baseline, +250, +375, +800 kaf/yr (800 kaf calibrated so the mean reaches 4,198 ft by 2055). 2024-version results (from 4,192.1 ft to 2054): baseline 4,191.4; +250 kaf 4,193.9; +375 kaf 4,195.1; +770 kaf 4,198.0. Report notes 2000-2025 inflow averaged 1,665 kaf/yr, ~2,465 kaf/yr needed for 4,198 by 2055; lake evaporation averaged 2,733 kaf/yr (1989-2024); +800 kaf/yr gives a 47% chance of the healthy level by 2034 (https://growtheflowutah.org/2026/01/08/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-great-salt-lake-strike-team-report/). No hindcast verification.
- USU Mass Balance Model (Dunn, Crookston, Dutta, Neilson 2025, J. Hydrol. Reg. Stud.; code/data https://hydroshare.org/resource/267e083484a74866927d91bdef60f8d6/): monthly water/salt balance for north arm, south upper and deep brine layers, Jan 2017-Sep 2023, with causeway breach exchange; a management tool, not a forecast. Error metrics in the paper (peer-reviewed, not extracted here).
- BYU "Emergency measures" report (Abbott et al., Jan 2023, https://pws.byu.edu/great-salt-lake): projected continued decline under 2020-2022 deficits (~1.2 million acre-ft/yr) with the lake possibly disappearing within ~5 years; deterministic extrapolation, no probabilistic model; widely criticised after the 2023 rebound.
- Lall et al. nonlinear short-term (few-hundred-day) volume forecasts and Sun/Chen ARFIMA/FIGARCH models exist but are academic and out of scope here.

## Takeaways for this project

1. The only routine operational lake-level outlook is NRCS's advisory rise-to-peak estimate (Jan-May), driven by a SNOTEL-regression inflow forecast. Its April error in 2024-2026 was 0.1-0.4 ft against a stated band of about plus or minus 0.5 ft. It is the natural benchmark for a spring-peak forecast.
2. CBRFC provides tributary ESP volumes with 16-18% April error but no lake product; those volumes and the NRCS GSL inflow exceedances are usable covariates.
3. Multi-year projections (USU Climate Center, Strike Team Monte Carlo, GSLIM) are scenario or climate-oscillation models. Reported RMSE for the USU statistical model is 1.2-1.5 ft (fit) to about 3 ft (8-yr out-of-sample); the Monte Carlo and GSLIM runs report no verification.
